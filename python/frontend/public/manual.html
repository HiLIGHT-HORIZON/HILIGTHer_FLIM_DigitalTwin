🔬 HILIGHTer Digital Twin Manual

A comprehensive guide to modelling, simulating, and optimising FLIM instrument architectures.

1. System Architecture

The Digital Twin is built on a three-layer orchestrator:

  • Physics Engine (Forward Model): Simulates IRF, Decay, and Gating non-idealities.
  • Estimation Layer (Inverse Problem): Implements High-speed MLE and Phasor mapping.
  • Precision Layer (CRLB): Calculates the absolute physical limits of the configuration.

2. Fisher Information & F-Value Theory

The F-value is the primary metric for evaluating instrument quality. It represents the SNR-normalized precision: the ratio of the achieved precision to the best possible precision constrained by Poisson statistics.

$$F = \frac{\sigma_\tau}{\tau} \sqrt{N}$$

Estimation Framework

The system calculates the Cramer-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB), which is the inverse of the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM). The FIM accounts for the coupling between all free parameters (e.g., how uncertainty in background affects lifetime precision):

FIM_uv = N * sum( (dP_i/dTheta_u * dP_i/dTheta_v) / P_i )
sigma_u = sqrt( inv(FIM)[u,u] )

Numerical Method: Detection probabilities $P_i$ are calculated by integrating the wrapped decay PDF with realistic Gaussian (Normal CDF) gate profiles. The edge slopes are defined by the standard deviation ($\sigma$) of the transition. The Jacobian $(\partial P / \partial \Theta)$ is estimated via central difference with $\epsilon=10^{-4}$.

3. Advanced Physics: Pulse Trains & Wrapping

In high-repetition systems (e.g., 80MHz), the molecular decay may not reach zero before the next laser pulse arrives.

PDF Wrapping: The backend handles this by modulo-summing the intensity over the repetition period ($T_{rep}$):

$$PDF_{wrapped}(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} PDF_{ideal}(t + k \cdot T_{rep})$$

This "afterglow" increases background bias and is essential for modeling realistic photon pile-up.

4. Theoretical Golden Reference

The Precision (Fisher Info) plot always displays a dashed reference curve (the "Golden Reference") evaluated from a physically perfect system. This system represents the absolute theoretical limit (the Ideal TCSPC) for your current acquisition window, assuming:

  • Dirac IRF: Zero-width excitation impulse at $t=0$.
  • Ideal Rectangular Gates: Perfectly sharp switching windows with zero rise-time.
  • Infinite Bandwidth: 256 high-resolution bins to establish the theoretical F-value ceiling.

Comparing your simulated instrument curve against this dashed line highlights the efficiency loss due to jitter, deadtime, and gating non-idealities.
