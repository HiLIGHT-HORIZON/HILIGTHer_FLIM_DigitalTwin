Figure 2

Figure 2. Monte Carlo simulations of time-gated FLIM with increasing width of an excitation pulse. At shorter excitation pulse widths, FLIM performs better, but performance degrades significantly only when the pulse width exceeds 5-7.5ns (i.e., up to 15% of duty cycle over a 50 ns period). On the left: photon-efficiency curves as a function of burst width (ns). On the right: diagrammatic representation of the simulated time gates and fluorescence emission signals.

Figure 3

Figure 3. Monte Carlo simulations of time-gated FLIM with increasing gate jittering/skewness. With increasing jittering/skewness, here modelled as a gaussian jittering with standard deviation from 0 to 1000ps, photon efficiency deteriorates. However, photon efficiency remains high for standard deviation values up to and beyond 500ps. On the left: photon-efficiency curves as a function of jittering. On the right: diagrammatic representation of the simulated time gates and fluorescence emission signals.
